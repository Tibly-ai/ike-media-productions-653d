How it works

Research to final cut

A documentary lives or dies in pre-production. We do the reading, the phone calls, and the pre-interviews first so that the shoot day is spent listening instead of scrambling.

What you receive

  • Finished documentary film, graded and mixed
  • Vertical and square cutdowns for social
  • Licensed music and clean interview audio
  • Stills pulled from the footage
  • Captions and delivery files for web, broadcast, or events
  1. 01

    Research and pre-interviews

    We learn the subject before a camera is in the room. Calls with the people involved, a look at any archive material, and a written outline of the story we think is there.

  2. 02

    Story plan and schedule

    Interview questions, a shot list for the b-roll that carries the narrative, locations, releases, and a day-by-day plan you sign off on. You always know what the film is trying to say.

  3. 03

    Production

    Cinema camera, shaped light, and two channels of audio on every interview. Crews stay small on purpose. People talk differently when there are three of us in the room instead of ten.

  4. 04

    Edit, sound, and color

    A paper edit first, then a rough cut for your notes. Two rounds of revisions are built in. The final pass covers color, sound mix, music licensing, and every deliverable size you need.

  5. 05

    Release plan

    Strategy sits next to the craft here. We map where the film runs, which cutdowns lead, and what each one is asking the viewer to do next.
